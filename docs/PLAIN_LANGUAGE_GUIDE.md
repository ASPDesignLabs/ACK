## How to use this document

If someone gave you this document — a link, a printout, a screen held up —
it's because they're using an app called **ACK** and, right now, talking out
loud isn't working for them. This document explains what that app is, in
plain terms, without requiring them to explain it to you themselves.

If you're the one who might hand this to someone: it's written to stand
alone. You shouldn't need to add anything for it to make sense.

## What ACK actually is

ACK is a phone app (with a companion Wear OS watch app) that plays prepared
phrases out loud and on screen, so a person who is temporarily unable to
speak can still communicate a specific message, on demand, in the moment
they need it.

It is a form of **AAC** — Augmentative and Alternative Communication, the
general term for any tool that supports or replaces spoken communication.
ACK is a non-traditional, more advanced take on that idea, built by one
person to solve a specific personal problem, and shared publicly as-is.

## Who this is for

ACK is built for someone who:

- has functional spoken and internal language most of the time, and
- experiences specific moments, situations, or stretches of time where
  getting that language out loud stops working, even though the words
  themselves are already fully formed.

The project's creator describes this as **situational mutism** — not a
vocabulary problem, not a knowledge problem, not a caring-or-not problem.
It's a bottleneck between a sentence being ready and that sentence actually
leaving the person's mouth. That gap can show up in ordinary, low-stakes
moments (replying when someone's waiting on an answer, starting a
conversation) or in higher-stakes ones (needing to ask something
time-sensitive and not being able to afford a freeze).

## Who this is *not* for

This is the part worth being unmistakable about:

**ACK is not built for, and is not a substitute for, a primary
communication system for someone with a developmental, cognitive, or
complex communication disability** — including nonverbal or
minimally-speaking autistic children and adults, or anyone whose spoken
language is not already reliably present to fall back on.

That is a different, well-established clinical space, with its own
purpose-built tools: core-vocabulary AAC systems (for example PODD or LAMP
Words for Life), consistent motor-planning design, caregiver-supported
implementation, and a formal AAC evaluation from a certified
speech-language pathologist. ACK's own creator has said directly that they
are not a speech-language pathologist, mental health professional, or
formally trained in AAC design — this was built to solve one person's
specific problem, not designed against that clinical body of practice.

**If you are a parent, caregiver, or clinician looking for a communication
support for someone with a significant, ongoing complex communication
need**, the right next step is a referral to a certified SLP for a formal
AAC evaluation — not this app, and not this document.

**ACK is also not a diagnostic tool, a treatment, or therapy**, and using
it is not a substitute for care from a qualified speech-language
pathologist or mental health professional.

## A note on related clinical terms

**[NEEDS REVIEW — this section makes claims that touch clinical/diagnostic
territory and should be checked by someone with relevant training before
this document is shared or published anywhere.]**

"Situational mutism" as used by ACK's creator is a descriptive, personal
term, not a formal diagnostic label. It overlaps with, but is not
identical to, several terms clinicians and researchers use:

- **Selective Mutism** — a DSM-5 anxiety-related diagnosis, most often
  first documented in childhood, characterized by a consistent inability
  to speak in specific social situations despite speaking comfortably in
  others (such as at home). Some adults continue to experience this
  pattern, though diagnostic pathways and terminology for adult
  presentations are less standardized than for children.
- **Autistic shutdown** — a temporary loss of reliable spoken output
  during sensory or cognitive overload, described within the autistic
  community as distinct from having an ongoing complex communication
  disability.
- **Panic-, freeze-, or dissociation-related mutism** — acute stress
  responses (panic attacks, trauma responses, dissociation) can also
  produce a temporary inability to speak.

None of these are the same thing, and this document does not claim ACK
addresses any of them specifically, diagnoses any of them, or is
recommended treatment for any of them. If any of this resonates with
someone, a speech-language pathologist, psychologist, or physician is the
right person to help sort out what's actually going on and what support —
which may or may not include a tool like ACK — actually helps.

## How it actually works

Every phrase in ACK can be triggered one of three ways. All three do the
same job — getting a prepared message out — at different costs of effort
and reach.

**Tap controls** — the baseline. Press a button in the app, the phrase
plays. Every other input method below is a faster or hands-free alternative
to this, not a replacement for it. If someone only ever uses tap controls,
the app is working exactly as intended.

**Gesture playback (the watch)** — a phrase can also be triggered from the
Wear OS companion watch app, without touching or unlocking the phone. This
matters when reaching for a phone, unlocking it, and finding the right
screen costs more than can be spared in the moment — hands full, mid-task,
or simply not wanting to interrupt what's happening to dig a phone out.
This is one additional way in, not the primary or only way to use ACK.

The watch uses a four-step sequence: **ARM → LOCK POSE → MODIFY → FIRE**.
"Arm" starts the sequence, "Lock Pose" confirms which gesture/phrase is
selected, "Modify" is an optional step to adjust it, and "Fire" is the step
where the phrase actually plays out loud and/or on screen. A single
accidental tap anywhere on the watch cancels the sequence before "Fire" —
starting the gesture does not commit to finishing it.

**Full-screen output** — when a phrase plays, it can show full-screen text
alongside spoken audio, so the other person gets the same context a spoken
sentence would give them, rather than being left to interpret a silent
screen. **Silent Mode** (found under PROTOCOL, see below) turns off the
audio half when sound isn't appropriate — a quiet office, a library — while
keeping the visual text.

## Feature glossary

Each entry below gives the exact wording as it appears in the app or on
the project site, followed by a plain-language explanation.

### Decks (general concept)

A **deck** is a tab holding a set of ready-made phrases organized around
one context — work, errands, emergencies, feelings. Rather than scrolling
through every phrase that has ever been saved, switching decks surfaces
only the phrases relevant to the current situation. Multiple decks can
exist side by side, and new ones can be created, renamed, recolored, or
removed at any time.

### MATRIX Deck

The main, general-purpose deck. It holds several broad categories, each
with a small set of ready phrases — enough range to rarely be stuck, small
enough to remember where things are. MATRIX decks also support
**profiles**, which let the same categories hold different phrasing for
different contexts (for example, work vs. home) without rebuilding the
whole layout.

### Quick Actions Deck

A smaller, fixed deck — up to twelve phrases, with no profile-switching.
Because nothing about it changes based on context, the same gesture or tap
always produces the same result. It exists for phrases that need to be
completely unmistakable regardless of situation: greetings, sign-offs,
fixed references.

### Emergency Deck

The high-priority deck, designed around one specific safety property: once
a message from this deck is on screen, it will not disappear on its own —
not from a stray touch, an involuntary movement, or a tremor. It only
clears when the person deliberately clears it. Messages from this deck can
also play an alert tone before the phrase itself, to increase the chance
it's noticed.

### Emoji Deck

A deck for sending a single emoji as a full reply, rather than a full
sentence, for situations where an emotional reaction is the message. The
built-in library is grouped by category (feelings, needs, boundaries)
rather than a generic emoji keyboard.

### GIF Deck

A deck for storing and replaying GIFs (short looping video clips, commonly
used for reactions online) as a form of reply, for someone who already
communicates that way in text and wants the same option in person.

### Manual Override

A plain text box for anything that doesn't fit an existing deck. Text
typed here can either be saved into a chosen category for reuse, or played
once and discarded if it isn't worth keeping.

### Target Computer

A way to store a small set of reusable details — a name, a place, a
specific identifying phrase — that can be inserted into an otherwise fixed
phrase instead of retyping it each time (for example, keeping "Thanks,
___" fixed while swapping in whichever name is needed).

### Audio Architect

The settings area controlling how ACK's synthesized voice sounds — pitch,
speed, volume, and texture — including the ability to build and save
custom voice presets. This is a settings/configuration area, not something
being actively used the moment its name appears on screen.

### Variables & Root Overrides

A way to avoid manually editing many saved phrases when one shared detail
changes. A phrase can hold a placeholder (a "variable") for something like
a name or date; filling that placeholder in once can update it everywhere
it's used, instead of editing each phrase individually.

### Geo-Protocol / Tactical Grid

**[NEEDS REVIEW — no existing plain-language description of this feature
exists anywhere in the project's public materials yet. The explanation
below was reconstructed from the in-app help system's source code, not
from the project creator's own words, and should be checked carefully for
accuracy before publishing.]**

Geo-Protocol is a location-based settings system: specific real-world
places can be set up as "zones," and being physically inside one can
change which phrases or decks are easiest to reach — for example, a
"grocery store" zone surfacing shopping-related phrases automatically.
**Tactical Grid** is simply the map view used to see and configure those
zones; no map data ships with the app by default, and zones work by raw
coordinate without one. A map file can optionally be imported later to see
an actual visual map underneath the zones.

Location detection for this feature uses Google's location services
(confirmed via the app's Android permissions and dependencies during this
review). Depending on the location accuracy setting chosen on the device,
this can mean querying Google's servers using nearby WiFi/cell data to
determine position, rather than using GPS alone — the one specific case
this project's own build notes already flagged as sending data off-device,
unlike the rest of the app, which has no internet permission at all and
otherwise runs fully offline. Worth double-checking which accuracy mode is
in use if this matters to someone reading this.

### Training Ground

A practice space for the watch gesture sequence where nothing actually
plays back or sends anywhere — a low-stakes place to build muscle memory
for the gesture before relying on it in a real moment.

### Deck Trainer

The same idea as Training Ground, but scored against the person's own
real deck layout, so practice matches what will actually be used rather
than a generic example.

### Wear OS companion (pairing & sync)

The watch app that senses gestures and sends them to the phone. It relies
on the watch already being paired to the phone through standard Wear OS
pairing — there is no separate ACK-specific setup step. The watch runs a
small background service so this keeps working without its screen needing
to stay on.

### ARM → LOCK POSE → MODIFY → FIRE

The four named steps of the watch gesture sequence described above under
"How it actually works." If any of these four words appear on a watch
screen, it reflects progress through that sequence — "FIRE" specifically
means the point where the selected phrase is about to play, not anything
related to weapons or danger.

### Shake Kill Switch

A safety feature: physically shaking the phone immediately stops whatever
is currently playing or on screen. It exists as a fast way to cut off
output if a gesture misfires or the wrong thing was tapped. The name
refers only to halting audio/visual playback — nothing else is affected.

### Shaky-Hands Mode

A setting that increases the time and tolerance the watch allows before
deciding a gesture is complete, intended for days when physical precision
is harder than usual — due to stress, fatigue, or any other reason.

### "A way out before it fires"

A safety behavior rather than a named feature: if a gesture sequence
starts but wasn't intended, a single tap anywhere on the watch cancels it
before anything plays. Starting a gesture does not commit to completing
it.

### REPLAY

A button that appears for about twenty seconds after a phrase is shown,
allowing it to be instantly replayed if it was missed, misheard, or not
seen. Anything said can also be replayed later from the Terminal log (see
below), beyond that twenty-second window.

### Terminal

A running, timestamped log of everything said or received during the
current session, primarily used to double-check what was actually sent or
to replay something from earlier in the session. It is not a permanent
history — it clears when the app is closed.

### PROTOCOL

**[NEEDS REVIEW — this term is used repeatedly across the project's
existing materials as a place to go configure something ("under PROTOCOL
from any deck"), but is never itself explicitly defined anywhere in the
source material reviewed for this draft. The description below is inferred
from context and should be confirmed.]**

PROTOCOL appears to be the name of the settings/options menu accessible
from within any deck, where features like Silent Mode, Shake Kill Switch
sensitivity, and Shaky-Hands Mode are configured.

### Your Data, Your Rules (backup & restore)

Everything created in ACK — decks, phrases, saved voices, settings — is
stored only on the device itself. A backup/export feature exists (under
Settings) so that data can survive a lost phone, factory reset, or device
change; without exporting a backup, that data exists in only one place.
