# Translations (INTERFACE LANGUAGE): what exists, what does not, and how to review it

**Status: five DRAFT translations, written by an AI assistant with no native speaker involved. None has been reviewed by a native speaker or a
speech-language pathologist (SLP), and none has been run on a phone.** Hindi and Arabic are the two the author is least sure of. Every
`values-xx/strings.xml` starts with a notice that says so, and the language picker says so too. Do not describe these as finished translations
anywhere, and do not remove the notice until a person who speaks the language has read the file.

| Language | Folder | Script / direction | Wording | What the author is least sure of |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spanish | `values-es` | Latin, left to right | Neutral, for Spain and Latin America; "computadora" for computer | Regional choices (computadora/ordenador, ajustes/configuración) |
| Portuguese | `values-pt` | Latin, left to right | **Brazilian** wording (tela, arquivo, celular, contato); European Portuguese would need its own `values-pt-rPT` review | Every Brazil/Portugal difference |
| Hindi | `values-hi` | Devanagari, left to right | Everyday Hindi with common English loan words (सेटिंग्स, बैकअप, इमोजी) | Which loan words people actually prefer; "खाली जगह" for a fill-in |
| Arabic | `values-ar` | Arabic, **right to left** | Modern Standard Arabic, no diacritics | Gender and plural agreement; "حقل" (field) for a fill-in; dialect-neutral tone |
| Afrikaans | `values-af` | Latin, left to right | Standard Afrikaans | Compounds (one word or two) and "'n" at the start of a line in capitals |

## What is translated

Only the **label table** and the words around PLAIN WORDS and INTERFACE LANGUAGE, because those are the words the developer gave the app:

- every name in `docs/PLAIN_LANGUAGE.md`, in both forms (`label_<key>` the standard name, `label_<key>_plain` the everyday name);
- the PLAIN WORDS switch, offer and explanation, and the plain-mode buttons and switches on the Terminal screen, the Type tab and in SETTINGS
  (`plain_words_*`, `plain_ctl_*`);
- the INTERFACE LANGUAGE control itself (`interface_language_*`, `common_cancel`); its heading is fixed text in all five languages so it can be found
  by someone who cannot read the current one;
- the accessibility-service description Android shows in its own settings;
- **screens, one at a time** (so each can be reviewed): the **Matrix deck screen** (group headings, ACTIVATE/ACTIVE, the shared-value strip and its dialog,
  the recorded badge, MANAGE CONTEXT), the **Matrix editor dialog** opened from a row (template, variables, target-tag fallbacks, the voice-recording panel
  text, the three CLEAR confirmations and the recording notices) the **Statement composer** (the TYPE tab: FULL SCREEN, the preview, SAVE / COPY / SPEAK, the save and new-folder dialogs, MY STATEMENTS and its row buttons) the **header row above every screen** (DECK, PROFILE, COMPUTER) the **People and places** category screens (the category grid, its status dialog, the category and entry dialogs, the tree and dropdown windows, GUIDED SETUP and the quick-insert browser) and the **contact cards** (PERSON CARD / PLACE CARD, their fields and examples, opening hours and the day names, and the two toasts; the brand names X, FACEBOOK and LINKEDIN are not translated) and the **Emergency deck** (its buttons, the overrides line and dialog, the large CONFIRM EMERGENCY dialog with SEND and CANCEL, and the EMERGENCY INFO card) and the **DELETE DATA wording** (the twelve areas and what each holds and how it is backed up, both confirmations, the amounts, what is said after a delete, the safety-copy rows and their two dialogs, and DELETE CUSTOM VOICE's two dialogs) and the **AUDIO ARCHITECT screen** (master gain, the profile chips, MANAGE PROFILES, the voice picker and its note, SPEECH LANGUAGE and its explanation, the custom voice buttons, the DSP chain editor, the import and restore messages and the one-time offer of the newer defaults) and the **MANAGE AUTOCOMPLETE dialog** and its SETTINGS section and the **MANAGE CONTEXT dialogs** (the list of layers, ADD, RENAME, REASSIGN and the delete question) and the **WORD SUGGESTIONS wording** (the SETTINGS switch and explanation, the count, FORGET WORDS and its confirmations, and the composer's one-time offer) and the **backup wording** (the EXPORT .JSON warning that the file is not encrypted and where not to save it,
  the backup reminder and its switch, why a backup failed, IMPORT MATRIX AS NEW DECK and the FULL RESTORE confirmation). Words used on several
  screens are `common_*`; a screen's own words are `<screen>_*` (`matrix_*`, `matrix_edit_*`, `composer_*`, `header_*`, `export_*`, `backup_*`, `data_port_*`). The decisions that choose
  those words (`core/ExportContents.kt`, `core/BackupReminderText.kt`) stay plain Kotlin and name them through a `TextSource`, so they are still tested against the real English text.
  DELETE DATA reports a delete by area **id** and says the areas' names in the chosen language, so no decision depends on a translated word. Its sentences that keep a delete safe (THIS CANNOT BE UNDONE, back up first, files saved elsewhere are not deleted, the restart and watch notes) are checked to be present in every language (`DeleteDataWordingTest`). A backup note names EXPORT .JSON and AUDIO ARCHITECT through the label table, so it reads like the button it points to; it still names **SAVE ALL TO A FILE, RECORD TRAINING DATA and EXPORT DECK (.ZIP) in English**, because those buttons are English on their own screens until those screens are migrated (change the notes and `DeleteDataWordingTest` together then). EXPORT VOICE BACKUP moved with the AUDIO ARCHITECT screen, so its note takes the button's name as an argument. The date of a safety copy uses the language's month names with Latin digits. DELETE DATA's area names do not follow PLAIN WORDS, as before.
  **People and places** follows the same rule as the Emergency buttons: the four categories a fresh install is given (PEOPLE, PLACES, FOOD/DRINK, ACTIONS) are saved text, and the ids made from them are what `[COMPUTER:id]` tokens refer to, so neither is ever changed. A category still carrying its saved default name is *shown* in the chosen language; one you renamed or made yourself is shown exactly as typed, and opening a category's options and saving without touching the name saves what was stored (`core/ComputerLabels.kt`). Entry names, and what is inserted into a message from the quick-insert browser, are always your own words. The message about "entries under PEOPLE" names the category the way it is shown. The count in the delete warning ("item(s)") is a plural, so each language gets the forms its grammar needs.
  **The Emergency deck** (decided with the developer): the deck, its overrides and the confirmation are in the chosen language only, and SEND and CANCEL are tested to be different words in every language. The **info card** is read by a bystander or first responder, so on the card *view* each label is the chosen language followed by English ("NOMBRE // NAME"; one label in English); the English half is fixed in `core/EmergencyLabels.kt` and a test keeps it equal to the English strings file. The edit form, which only the person sees, uses the chosen language alone. What is written on the card is never touched. A button still named by its default (`EMERGENCY 1`..`4`) is *shown* in the chosen language while the saved name stays the default (editing and saving without touching the name saves what was stored); a name you typed is shown as typed. **The saved communication note ("I am non-verbal or unable to speak right now...") is not translated**: it is saved text a bystander reads, so it stays as saved and you can edit it. A translated default note for new installs would be a separate decision.
  **AUDIO ARCHITECT** (decided with the developer): **CYBER, MECH, ORGANIC and MY VOICE are names, not words to translate**, and so is the CUSTOM A, B... name a new slot is saved with. They read the same in every language, so the chips, the home-screen widget, HELP and every translated sentence agree. A sentence that mentions MY VOICE takes the name as `%1$s` (`CustomVoiceRemoval.MY_VOICE_LABEL`) instead of retyping it, and `AudioScreenWordingTest` checks every language does that. Translating the names for display, the way the Emergency button names are, is possible later but would first need the widget, HELP and the Terminal to follow. "Slot" has no settled word yet in the drafts (casilla, vaga, plek, स्लॉट, خانة): a reviewer should choose. The two ON / OFF buttons in the DSP chain editor are at least 60 dp wide and grow with a longer word (DESACTIVADO), instead of a fixed 60 dp. Units that are not words stay in the code beside the translated name: (HZ) and (%). **The offer of the newer defaults** (`core/DefaultsText.kt`, `settings/DefaultsPrompt.kt`) picks one of three banner sentences by what is on offer, passes ORGANIC and CYBER in as names, names EXPORT .JSON the way the SETTINGS button reads at that moment (its everyday name with PLAIN WORDS on), and keeps **ALERT:, FULL TEXT and SHOW FULL MESSAGE in English in every language**: the first is what the display shows, the second is the saved name of the preset the offer adds, the third is the visual editor's switch (the editor is not translated yet; change the sentence and `DefaultsWordingTest` together when it is).
  Two things must survive translation exactly: the typed command in `export_terminal_confirm` (`/backup CONFIRM`, which the Terminal accepts) and the `%1$s` / `%2$s` placeholders. A count of days
  is a `<plurals>` entry, so each language gets the forms its grammar needs (Arabic has six). **WORD SUGGESTIONS** (`core/WordSuggestionText.kt`, `settings/WordSuggestionsSection.kt`): the sentences name the screens and buttons they point to as arguments (EXPORT .JSON, TARGET COMPUTER, FORGET WORDS), so a sentence and the button read the same in every language; SHARED VARIABLES is written in each language with the same words as that screen's own name in the label table (the English sentence has always said SHARED VARIABLES, not its full name SHARED ROOT VARIABLES). FORGET ALL WORDS' second confirmation and the "files saved elsewhere" sentence are read from the very same strings as DELETE DATA's, so they cannot differ. The count of times a word was used and the count removed are plurals. The suggestion strip itself has no words of its own: it shows your own words. **MANAGE CONTEXT** (`ui/ManageContextDialog.kt`, moved out of `DesignSystem.kt` unchanged so it can be type-checked): the pose names in it (the permanent rows, BASED ON, the pose picker) go through `poseLabel()` like the Matrix screen, but the stored pose names are still what is saved and compared; the name of a layer you typed is shown exactly as typed (names are still capitalised and capped at 24 characters as you type). The word POSE has no screen of its own yet, so the sentences that say it use the label table's word for it. The delete question keeps both parts (what is lost across every deck and profile, and the advice to export a backup first) in every language. **MANAGE AUTOCOMPLETE** (`settings/ManageAutocompleteDialog.kt`, `core/AutocompleteLabels.kt`): the tree is built in a plain function, so it is handed a `TextSource` instead of calling a composable; what groups a branch and remembers whether it is open is still the stored deck, profile, pose and category, and only the label is a word. A pose is shown the way the Matrix screen shows it; a layer, deck, profile, group, slot or node name you gave, and every remembered value, is shown as typed. A pose that cannot be found is grouped under `?` (which cannot be a layer's name) and shown as UNKNOWN, so a layer you call UNKNOWN is never mistaken for it. `VAR:A` is token syntax and is not translated. The tree shows the standard names for MATRIX, QUICK ACTIONS, SHARED ROOT VARIABLES and VARIABLE whatever PLAIN WORDS says (as DELETE DATA does): a gap, not a decision, noted in `PlainWordsScreensWiringTest`. The two clear questions each keep their sentences (what is removed, nothing else is affected, cannot be undone) and the dialog still asks once for each clear, as it always did. The shared dialog frame's own [CLOSE] (`ui/OverlayStyle.kt`) is the `common_close` resource, so the dialogs that do not name their own word say it in the chosen language; three dialogs in `DesignSystem.kt` and `ManageRecordingsDialog.kt` pass their own literal CLOSE, ABORT and CANCEL until those screens are migrated. Still to come: the HELP menu.

## What is NOT translated (it stays English in every language, and the control says so)

HELP walkthroughs and their menu; every screen not listed above (and the deck manager); the Terminal's own text (`/help`, the patch notes, command feedback); most dialogs, toasts and settings captions;
the Terminal's own replies; the visual overlay and anything spoken (that is SPEECH LANGUAGE's job); starter phrases; anything a person typed.
A Spanish screen is therefore mixed: translated names on the main screens, English in the long tail. That is the stated limit of this pass.

## How it behaves

- **An install that already existed stays English** until its owner chooses a language in SETTINGS, even on a phone set to Spanish. A **new install
  follows the phone**, and so does a phone after DELETE DATA > SETTINGS (its confirmation says so). A phone language ACK has no words for shows English.
- Choosing asks first (CANCEL is the prominent button), then saves the choice and **restarts ACK once**. Nothing is deleted. The language is applied in
  `MainActivity.attachBaseContext` (`data/InterfaceLocale.kt`); if that ever fails ACK still opens, in English.
- The choice is in EXPORT .JSON (`interfaceLanguage`, nullable: a backup that says nothing leaves your own choice alone).
- **Android 13's own per-app language screen is deliberately not offered** (no `localeConfig`): an existing install is held on English by the in-app
  setting, and a second switch would silently lose to it. A test fails if one is declared.
- **Letter spacing is dropped in Arabic** (`ui/ScriptSpacing.kt`): the app's wide spacing is a style for capital Latin letters, and it pulls an Arabic word
  apart. Every `letterSpacing = N.sp` goes through `looseSpacing(...)`; a test fails on one that does not.

## What a reviewer should do

1. Open `values-xx/strings.xml`. Each `label_<key>` is the **standard** name (ACK's own jargon, translated closely) and `label_<key>_plain` the **everyday**
   name (what a first-time user or an SLP should see). The everyday name matters most; the English reasoning is in `docs/PLAIN_LANGUAGE.md`.
2. Change the text freely. **Keep the keys, keep the `%1$s` placeholders, escape an apostrophe as `\'`.** Spanish, Portuguese and Afrikaans stay in capitals
   (the app's house style); Hindi and Arabic have none.
3. Keep the structure the tests enforce: every everyday name differs from its standard name (except `NAV_TYPE`, `DECK_TYPE_EMERGENCY`, `DECK_TYPE_EMOJI`),
   and no two buttons share an everyday name except the four same-place pairs (HISTORY, PEOPLE AND PLACES, LOCATION ALERTS, VOICE AND SOUND).
4. Remove the DRAFT notice at the top only when you have read the whole file. Run `bash tools/kotlin_check/run_unit_tests.sh` (`TranslationsTest`).

To **add a language**: add it to `InterfaceLanguage` (`core/InterfaceLanguage.kt`), add `values-xx/strings.xml`, and update `TranslationsTest`/`InterfaceLanguageTest`
(they pin the five). The control lists whatever `InterfaceLanguage` holds.

## Not verified (no Android SDK, nothing run on a phone)

The restart and the locale applied through `createConfigurationContext`; every translated screen's layout (longer words wrapping in 48 dp buttons); the
mirrored layout in Arabic; which digits appear (most numbers are built by the app with Kotlin templates and show 0 to 9, but a value written with `String.format`, such as the twist-sensitivity slider, follows the phone's own number format); how the monospace font falls back for Devanagari and Arabic glyphs; TalkBack in each language; Android's own lint for resources.
`docs/LANGUAGE_VOCABULARY_DEVICE_TEST.md` section K is the do-this-expect-that list.
